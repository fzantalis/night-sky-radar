## Task 1: Streaming fetch

Added `tlefetcher::fetchGroupStreaming()` in `src/TleFetcher.{h,cpp}`, alongside the
existing `fetchGroup()` (untouched — `stations`/`visual` still use it). It reads from
`http.getStreamPtr()` line-by-line into a fixed 160-byte stack buffer, holds at most one
three-line element set (`char triple[3][160]`) at a time, parses each with the existing
`parseTle()`, and offers it to a caller-supplied filter. Nothing scales with body size:
`Tle` is a plain-old-data struct (fixed char arrays, no `std::string`), there are no
`String` allocations in the hot loop, and the accepted-results vector is capped at
`maxKeep`. The stream is always drained to completion (or transport failure) regardless
of whether the cap was hit, both so `HTTPClient` tears down cleanly and so the raw
triple count (`totalParsed`) is a meaningful "did we get the real catalog" signal
independent of how many the filter kept.

I also deleted the false claim flagged in the plan: `parseTleText()`'s old comment said
holding three lines at a time is "what makes the 1.2 MB Starlink group tractable" — false,
since that function's `raw` parameter is already a fully-materialised `String` by the time
it runs. The comment now says so explicitly and points at `fetchGroupStreaming()` instead.

**Peak-heap evidence.** The real `starlink` endpoint was rate-gated for this whole session
(see below), so the live heap measurement is against the real `visual` endpoint (157
objects, ~26 KB body) through the *same* streaming code path, temporarily substituted in
place of `starlink` for one verification pass, then reverted before the final build:

```
[tle] streaming fetch of 'visual': 157 triple(s) parsed, 1 kept, min free heap 215124 bytes
```

Free heap at boot was ~275–284 KB; the floor during the fetch was 215,124 bytes — a
~60–68 KB drop, consistent with the ~40–50 KB `WiFiClientSecure`/`HTTPClient` TLS
overhead already documented in this file, not with body size. Because nothing in the loop
allocates per line or per triple (fixed buffers, POD `Tle`, capped output vector), the
same floor is expected running the identical loop 10,721 times (the real `starlink`
count, confirmed by direct analysis below) instead of 157 times. This is an inference from
code inspection plus a live test of the identical mechanism, not a live 1.7 MB-scale
measurement — see the hardware verification section for why, and what would close that
gap.

## Filter: mean motion, verified against real data, corrected once

Fetched the actual CelesTrak `starlink` group directly (`curl`, 2026-09-04, 1.80 MB,
32,163 lines, 10,721 objects) and analysed the full mean-motion distribution before
choosing anything:

- Median mean motion 15.317 rev/day; 8,441 of 10,721 objects (79%) round to 15.3 —
  this is the settled operational cluster the plan predicted (~15.06 was the plan's
  estimate for 550 km; the real figure the constellation actually sits at is closer to
  15.3, but the *shape* of the argument — a tight operational cluster with a gap above
  it — holds).
- **15.35 rev/day** (the plan's suggested threshold) sits just above that cluster and
  below every still-climbing object in the fetch. At that threshold + "launched this
  year or last" (2025 or 2026): **333 objects** matched.

**Spec correction found during verification, beyond the one already in the plan.**
Of those 333, ~280 were Starlink "Direct to Cell" satellites (CelesTrak tags them
`[DTC]` in the name) sitting permanently at 15.70–15.71 rev/day across **15+ different
launches from January 2026-09-04** — not because they were recently launched, but because
DTC satellites fly lower than the rest of the constellation *by design*. Mean motion
alone cannot tell "still climbing toward 550 km" apart from "operates permanently at
~350 km"; the year window doesn't help either, since DTC launches span the whole
current+previous year. Worse: DTC objects have **lower NORAD catalog numbers** than the
one genuine train in this fetch, and CelesTrak's group file is sorted by catalog number —
so a naive `maxKeep=80` cap would fill entirely with 8-month-old DTC stragglers and
**never reach the real train**, which carried the highest catalog numbers (69975–69998)
in the entire 10,721-object group.

Fix: `trainfilter::isTrainCandidate()` (`lib/core/src/TrainFilter.{h,cpp}`) additionally
excludes any name containing `DTC`, on top of the plan's mean-motion+year test. This is
a name-based exclusion, not a threshold change — I did not touch 15.35.

**What the corrected filter actually selects (2026-09-04 fetch):** 53 objects total.
Grouping by launch designator shows exactly one coherent train:

| designator | members | mean motion range |
|---|---|---|
| **2026-159** | **24** | 15.7518–15.7540 rev/day |
| 2025-018 | 2 | 15.3759–15.5467 |
| 2026-123 | 2 | 15.3540–15.3541 |
| 2026-147 | 2 | 15.7528–15.7533 |
| 2026-154 | 3 | 15.3539–15.3542 |
| 2026-157 | 3 | 15.3541–15.3542 |
| 13 other designators | 1 each | 15.35–15.98 |

2026-159's 24 members sit within a 0.0022 rev/day band of each other and carry the
highest NORAD catalog numbers in the whole group (69975–69998) — the signature of a
single batch deployed together and still climbing in formation. Sample members:
`STARLINK-38024` (69975), `STARLINK-37993`, `STARLINK-38057`, … `STARLINK-38091`
(69997). The other 29 objects are one-or-two-member stragglers scattered across 15
unrelated, mostly months-old launches — plausibly residual station-keeping/maneuver
noise, not visible trains. I did not filter these out further: `MAX_TRAIN=80` comfortably
holds all 53, and Task 3's "group by launch designator" roster naturally shows them as
their own tiny 1–3-member groups rather than false-conflating them with 2026-159.

**Native tests** for the predicate live in `test/test_trainfilter/test_trainfilter.cpp`:
threshold boundary, the year window (current/previous/two-years-back/future), the DTC
exclusion, a custom-threshold override, and three regression cases pinned to the real
`STARLINK-38024` / `STARLINK-11694 [DTC]` / `STARLINK-1008` objects from this exact
fetch.

## Task 2: Wiring into the scope

- `ScopeService::attemptRefresh()` fetches `starlink` via the streaming path, after
  `stations` and `visual`, still serialised (one `WiFiClientSecure` at a time, matching
  the existing constraint).
- **Important fix found during hardware testing, not in the original plan-following
  draft:** my first implementation gated `rebuildFromGroups()`/`markFetched()` on *all
  three* groups succeeding. Live testing immediately exposed why that's wrong — see
  "Hardware verification" below: a `starlink`-only failure would have taken
  `stations`/`visual` down with it, directly violating done-criterion 4 ("existing
  stations and visual tracking is unaffected"). Fixed: `stations`/`visual` success alone
  gates the rebuild and the freshness timestamp, exactly as before M3. `starlink`
  shares their cadence (fetched in the same cycle, after both) but neither blocks nor
  is blocked by them; on its own failure it falls back to whatever train roster is
  already cached on flash rather than erasing it.
- Plausibility (`tlecount::tleCountIsPlausible`) is checked against the **raw** parsed
  count (thousands of objects), never the filtered/accepted count — the latter is
  legitimately zero most days, and the existing plausibility gate rejects `count<=0`
  unconditionally (by design, for `stations`/`visual`, which should never be empty).
  Reusing it on the filtered count would treat every zero-train day as a failure.
- `MAX_TRAIN = 80` caps `fetchGroupStreaming()`'s output (real data needs 53).
  `MAX_TRACKED` raised 220→300 to leave room for it alongside the existing ~175
  stations+visual objects.
- Filtered results are cached via the *existing* `TleStore` (group `"starlink"`,
  temp-and-rename, unchanged) by re-serialising the already-parsed, already-filtered
  `Tle` entries back to three-line text (`Tle::name`/`line1`/`line2` are retained
  verbatim from the original fetch, so this is lossless).
- `Blip` gained `kind` (`"sat"`/`"train"`, default `"sat"`) and `launchYear`/
  `launchNumber` (populated for every blip, needed so the renderer can group trains by
  designator without guessing from the name string).

## Task 3: Rendering (`data/radar.js`)

- Train blips draw as a small diamond instead of a circle, plus a thin connecting line
  through all currently-drawn train members (sorted by sky radius) — the point is that
  they move together, so the connector is what actually shows that. Colour logic is
  unchanged (`P.text` / `P.visible`); `#d8f4ff` is still used only for `visible: true`,
  train or not.
- The roster groups train members by `launchYear`-`launchNumber` into one row
  ("`TRAIN 2026-159 ×24`", elevation range, representative bearing) instead of one row
  per satellite. Non-train blips are unaffected.

## Hardware verification

Board: COM6, `192.168.31.60` (confirmed unchanged via serial log on every boot).

1. **Firmware + filesystem build and flash cleanly.** `pio run -e esp32-s3-devkitc-1`
   succeeds (RAM 16.0%, Flash 19.1%); `-t upload` and `-t uploadfs` both succeeded
   multiple times during this session.
2. **Streaming mechanism verified live**, temporarily substituting `visual` for
   `starlink` in one build (reverted before the final build — confirmed by `git diff`
   showing no trace of it): `157 triple(s) parsed, 1 kept, min free heap 215124 bytes`,
   `plausible=1, saved=1`. Proves the line-by-line read, fixed-buffer parsing, filter
   callback, heap tracking, and cache round-trip all work against a real HTTPS
   connection end to end.
3. **The real `starlink` endpoint returned HTTP 403 to this network for this entire
   session**, with an explicit, documented reason in the response body:
   ```
   GP data has not updated since your last successful download
   of GROUP=starlink at 2026-09-04 18:13:31 UTC.
   Data is updated once every 2 hours.
   ```
   That "last successful download" was my own research `curl` fetch of the same
   endpoint (used for the threshold analysis above), made *before* any device testing
   began. CelesTrak's 2-hour freshness window means this network cannot get a fresh
   `starlink` response again until ~20:13 UTC — confirmed independently with a plain
   `curl` from this machine (`stations` returns 200, `starlink` returns 403, same
   moment). This is CelesTrak's own documented dedup policy, not rate-limiting abuse
   protection and not a bug in the streaming code.
4. **Failure path verified live, twice, including the critical fix.** Before the
   all-three-groups-gated draft was fixed: a wiped cache (`uploadfs`) plus the 403
   produced `[scope] refresh failed, retrying in 120 s` and **zero tracked objects** —
   i.e. `stations`/`visual`, which had just fetched and saved successfully, were
   discarded because of `starlink` alone. This is exactly the done-criterion-4
   violation described above, caught by hardware testing rather than review. After the
   fix, the identical scenario (wiped cache, `starlink` still 403) produced:
   ```
   [tle] fetched 3528 bytes for group stations
   [tle] fetched 26376 bytes for group visual
   [tle] streaming GET failed, code 403
   [scope] starlink streaming fetch: 0 triple(s) parsed, -1 kept as train candidates, min free heap 0 bytes, plausible=0, saved=0
   [scope] tracking 175 objects, psram free 8083131
   ```
   and `/api/scope` confirmed `"status":"ok"`, `"tleAgeHours":0.01`, 12 blips, all
   `"kind":"sat"` — stations/visual fresh and serving, train roster correctly empty
   (zero, not corrupted or stale-but-mislabelled) because this cycle's fetch failed and
   no prior successful `starlink` cache existed yet.
5. **Device left running the final, correct firmware and filesystem** (no diagnostic
   leftovers — confirmed via `git diff`/`grep` before the last build), with
   `stations`/`visual` tracking live and correct. `starlink` will retry automatically on
   the normal 12-hour cadence; the CelesTrak window reopens today at ~20:13 UTC, after
   which the next natural or forced (`uploadfs`) refresh cycle will exercise the real
   1.7 MB path end to end.

### What this leaves unverified

The one thing I could not directly observe this session: the actual `min free heap`
number during a *real* ~1.7 MB, 10,721-object `starlink` streaming fetch. The argument
that it will match the 157-object measurement (no per-iteration allocation; every buffer
is fixed-size; the only per-iteration heap-affecting call is `push_back` into a
`maxKeep`-capped vector) is sound but unverified live. If someone re-runs this after
20:13 UTC (or from a different network), the `[tle] streaming fetch of 'starlink':
... min free heap N bytes` log line is the number to check against ~215,000 (allowing
some margin — expect it to land close to the same floor, not meaningfully lower).

## Deviations from the plan

- Added a DTC-name exclusion to the filter beyond the plan's literal mean-motion+year
  test, justified with real data (see above) — verified independently before changing
  anything, as instructed.
- `fetchGroupStreaming()`'s signature carries two extra optional out-parameters
  (`totalParsed`, `minFreeHeapOut`) beyond the plan's sketch, needed for the
  plausibility gate and the heap-reporting requirement respectively; both default to
  `nullptr` and are backward-compatible with the plan's signature.
- Decoupled `starlink`'s success from `stations`/`visual`'s (see above) — not something
  the plan called out explicitly, but required by done-criterion 4 and caught only by
  running on real hardware.
- Could not capture a live full-scale (1.7 MB) successful `starlink` fetch in this
  session due to CelesTrak's own freshness-dedup policy (see above), triggered by my
  own research fetch earlier in the session. Documented exactly what was and wasn't
  verified rather than fabricating a number.

## Follow-up (controller, same evening)

CelesTrak still returns **HTTP 403** for `GROUP=starlink` from this network, so the
full-scale (~1.7 MB) streaming heap figure remains inferred rather than measured. The body
of the 403 is 143 bytes and states CelesTrak's own freshness policy — it refuses a repeat
download of a group whose GP data has not changed since your last successful fetch.

**This is not a defect and will not affect normal operation.** The device fetches on a
12-hour cadence, far outside CelesTrak's dedup window; only our repeated same-day testing
tripped it. The next scheduled device cycle will exercise the real path.

What IS established: the streaming mechanism works end to end on hardware against a real
CelesTrak body, peak free internal heap held at 215,124 bytes, and nothing in the
implementation scales with body size (fixed 160-byte line buffers, POD `Tle`, capped output
vector). The filter was validated against the genuine 10,721-object catalogue fetched
earlier in the session.
